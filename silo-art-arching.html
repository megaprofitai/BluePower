What Does Arching Mean?

Arching is the formation of an arch-shaped structure across the outlet. The arch is self-supporting and nothing passes through. It is particularly common in conical hoppers. Arching can be mechanical, where large particles physically block the opening, or cohesive, where fine materials are held together by cohesive forces.

Why Arching Occurs

Cohesive strength between particles is the primary driving force. Consolidation pressure affects arch strength, the hopper half-angle determines the pressure profile, and wall friction affects pressure distribution.

Consequences of Arching

Complete production stoppage. Safety incidents from manual intervention. Unpredictable flow: when the arch breaks, the entire material column can drop and overload the discharge system. Lost raw materials and production.

Limitations of Common Methods

Increasing the outlet diameter is correct but requires expensive rebuilding. Air cannons break the arch up temporarily, but it reforms. Vibrators are rarely powerful enough for high cohesion. Smoother walls reduce friction but not cohesion.

Mechanical Solution to Arching

BinWhip technology breaks up established arches without personnel needing to enter the silo. The tool breaks down material systematically for controlled discharge. By removing deposits from walls and the cone area, the conditions that promote arch formation are reduced.
