Product quality and food safety impose strict requirements on hygiene and cross-contamination. Common Challenges in Feed & Grain

Caking and lump formation — grain and meal absorb moisture and clump in layers along silo walls

Insect and pest infestation in dead material that has remained too long

Mould formation (aflatoxins, ochratoxin) in humid stagnant zones — serious health hazard

Cross-contamination between batches when old material breaks loose unpredictably

Ratholing causing FIFO violations — oldest material does not come out first

Reduced silo capacity forcing expensive interim storage solutions

Bridging over outlets, especially with fine-grained products like meal and premixes

Why It Happens

Feed and grain block silos through biological and physical processes:

Moisture migration

Temperature differences between grain and silo walls drive moisture towards cooler surfaces. This creates condensation zones where grain bonds to the wall.

Biological degradation

When grain moisture exceeds 14%, mould growth accelerates. Mycelium binds particles together forming hard masses.

Fine fractions

Meal, dust and premixes have very low flowability. They pack under pressure and can form cement-like blockages.

Protein content

Soy meal and other protein-rich raw materials are especially prone to caking, because proteins act as natural binders when moist.

Silo size

Large grain silos (30–50 metres tall) generate enormous pressure at the bottom layer. Material compresses and loses flowability.
