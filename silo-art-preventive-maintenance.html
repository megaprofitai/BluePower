What Is Preventive Maintenance?

Preventive maintenance involves planned, systematic measures to maintain equipment function and prevent unexpected failures. For silos, this means regular cleaning, inspection of structural elements, testing of safety equipment, and monitoring of material flow. The goal is to identify and resolve problems before they cause production disruptions.

Components of a Maintenance Programme

Planned cleaning

Intervals based on material type and operating conditions.

Structural inspection

Walls, foundations, and support structures.

Instrument testing

Level sensors and load cells verified against actual content.

Discharge equipment

Maintenance of screws, valves and other discharge components.

Seals and hatches

Inspection of seals and inspection hatches.

Ventilation

Check of ventilation systems and filters.

Costs of Reactive vs. Preventive Maintenance

Reactive maintenance — waiting until something fails — is always more expensive. Emergency cleaning of a blocked silo typically costs 3–5 times more than planned cleaning. Additionally, production losses can amount to tens or hundreds of thousands per day depending on the industry. Preventive maintenance provides predictable costs and eliminates most emergencies.

Documentation and Traceability

Good documentation is essential for an effective maintenance programme. Log all cleanings, inspections, and incidents with date, scope, and findings. This provides a historical basis for optimising intervals and identifying trends. For the food industry, documentation is also a regulatory requirement for HACCP and food safety.

Condition-Based Maintenance

Modern sensor technology enables condition-based maintenance, where cleaning is triggered by actual conditions in the silo rather than fixed intervals. Level sensors, temperature gauges, and differential pressure sensors can all provide valuable information about buildup and flow problems. This further optimises maintenance intervals.
