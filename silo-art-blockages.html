What Is a Silo Blockage?

A silo blockage occurs when bulk material can no longer flow out through the outlet as intended. This can manifest as complete stoppage or uneven, intermittent discharge. A blockage is a symptom of an imbalance between material properties, silo design, and operating conditions. In an industrial context, it is one of the most costly problems a facility can experience.

The Most Common Causes of Silo Blockages

Material cohesion

Materials with high cohesion clump together and form structures that resist gravity flow.

Moisture and temperature

Changes can dramatically alter flow properties.

Incorrect silo design

Many silos are designed without adequate consideration of the material’s actual flow properties.

Storage time and consolidation

Material gradually compacts under the weight above.

Segregation

Different particle sizes can create zones with different flow properties.

Consequences of Silo Blockages

Economic losses

From production downtime, lost materials, and overtime costs.

Safety risks

Attempts at manual removal are the most frequent cause of serious silo accidents.

Material loss

From degradation and contamination.

Structural damage

From uneven loading.

Environmental issues

From uncontrolled material release.

Traditional Solution Methods

Poking and rodding

Effective for minor blockages but dangerous and limited.

Air cannons and vibration

Can prevent, but are rarely effective against established blockages.

Manual entry

The most dangerous method — it should be avoided entirely.

Chemical treatment

Can alter material properties and is not always compatible.
